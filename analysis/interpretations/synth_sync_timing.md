The timing block closes timing out of context at 100 MHz on the xc7a100t-1 after place and route, with no failing endpoints (slack in the CSV). It uses 9 DSP48E1s and no block RAM, since the 4-sample window is in registers. From the synthesis DSP report: four for the six Horner products of the two rails, two that Vivado chose for c3 = 3(p1 − p2) + (p3 − p0) (pre-adder and ×3, one per rail), one for the Gardner product with its rounding constant, one for ki·e with the integrator add, and one for kp·e. Two stages were split for timing, each costing one clock per output without changing any output. First, the last Horner rounding, halving, add and saturation were in one cycle (14 logic levels, +0.583 ns); split, the block alone reached +0.745 ns. Second, inside `sync_top` the cubic's coefficient adders feeding the Horner DSP were the chain's worst path at +0.525 ns. Computing partial sums first and the coefficients a cycle later raised the block to +1.604 ns and the chain to +1.431 ns. The worst path is now a coefficient register into the first Horner DSP, two logic levels. Out-of-context results exclude I/O and clocking.
