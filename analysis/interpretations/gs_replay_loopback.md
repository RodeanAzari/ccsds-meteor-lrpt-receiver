`gs-replay` sent the whole synthetic recording to 127.0.0.1, where a Python receiver checked each datagram's sequence and hashed the payload. At real time and at 5×, all 68,266 datagrams arrived in order and the reassembled IQ was byte-identical to the file. The achieved rates were 1.00× and 5.00×. The `late_packets` column counts datagrams sent more than one period after their deadline. That is WSL scheduler jitter and it varies between runs: across four runs on 2026-11-04 it was 69–304 at 1× (≤ 0.5%) and 1,384–4,880 at 5× (2–7%, where a period is 100 µs). Deadlines are absolute, so the average rate still comes out exact, but late datagrams go out in short bursts. Flat-out ran at 61–124× real time, and the Python receiver lost between 0.4% and 17% of datagrams across runs, at over 100,000 per second. That is the measuring receiver's limit, not a sender fault, and over the 100 Mb/s link to the Arty flat-out is capped at about 5.7× anyway (`docs/protocol.md`). Each run's final 740 bytes, less than one datagram, were reported and not sent. None of this measures the Ethernet link or the board; that needs eth-core-v0.1.
